I work in Intesa Sanpaolo, an italian bank based in Milan. I'm in the XVA Management desk which prices and hedges the x value adjustments. 

I often get asked what does XVA Management entail, and I haven't found a simple manner to explain it, so read on if you're curious, I will do the simplest explanation possibile.

Banks and non-financial clients often agree on non-collateralized OTC derivatives, for example [swaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swap_(finance)). These derivatives change in value during their lifetime, a change given by the movements of the financial markets. This is called market risk. But there is another risk, the risk that one of the counterparties fails and is not able to repay the amount it ows, this risk can be calculated and assigned a monetary value, called CVA. This CVA values during the life of the derivative contract and so must be hedged. Where hedging means buying correlated instruments in order to counterbalance the price swings caused by the derivative. This is just one example of a VA, there are several other VAs, so together they make the [xVAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVA).

So in general during my work-day, I need to price the xVA's of derivate contracts and hedge them, as well as hedge the portfolio of already existing derivatives.
